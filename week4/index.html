Pretend we know the truth

Set a true prevalence p₀. Each simulated study runs a survey from p₀, finds its best estimate p̂, and measures how much better p̂ fits than the truth:

S = 2 × [log L(p̂) − log L(p₀)]

S above 3.84 is the same as the log-likelihood dropping more than 1.92 from p̂ to the truth. With plenty of data it happens in about 5% of studies, whatever p₀ is. With very little data the theory curve is only a rough guide.

Draw a line, get an interval

Drag the green line up and down the chart (or use the slider). Every value of p where the curve is above the line is in the interval. The lower the line, the wider the interval, and the more often it catches the truth. On the log scale the line sits a drop below the top; on the likelihood scale it sits at a % of the peak.
