Survival Lab

A study of malaria reinfection in 100 people. Each person is treated, which clears the parasites, and joins the study that day. People join steadily from day 0 to day 300. Each is followed until they are reinfected or until the study stops on day 365. Half sleep under a bed net. How long does it take to be reinfected?

The Kaplan-Meier curve

The study as it is when it stops on day 365. Kaplan-Meier uses everyone, for as long as they were followed.

On any day, the people at risk are those still free and still being followed. On each day with a reinfection, the proportion surviving the day is 1 − reinfected / at risk, and the curve (the cumulative survival, S(t)) is multiplied by it. Survival here means staying free of malaria.

A censored person counts as at risk up to their last day of follow-up, then leaves without making the curve drop.

You can also drag the reading line on the plot.

Bed nets and the hazard

A Kaplan-Meier curve for each group: the 50 people who don't sleep under a bed net (blue) and the 50 who do (green).

The hazard is the rate of reinfection among people still free: roughly, the chance of being reinfected tomorrow if you haven't been yet.

A Cox model sums up the difference between the groups in one number, the hazard ratio (HR): at every time, the hazard for net users is the hazard for non-users multiplied by the HR.

The dashed green line is the no-net curve with every day's reinfection rate multiplied by the HR. On each day, proportion surviving = 1 − HR × reinfected / at risk, using the people without a net.

You can also drag the reading line on the plot.
